Fifteen multiple-choice questions in the style of the final exam (40 MCQs, 60 minutes). One correct option each. Questions 1 to 5 are the module's own knowledge check; 6 to 15 are additional. The **Outcome** tag names the curriculum "Key Outcomes" bullet each question assesses.

## Questions

**1.** A step should be classed as rule-based when...
A. It is done by a senior person
B. You can write the complete rule in a sentence with no exceptions
C. It involves an email
D. It saves the most time

**2.** What should happen to an approval that is always given without reading (a RUBBER step)?
A. Automate it so it is faster
B. Assign it to an AI step
C. Remove it, or turn it into an exception check
D. Add a second approver

**3.** Which factor is NOT one of the four risk questions in this module?
A. Reversibility
B. Money
C. Popularity with staff
D. Legal and data

**4.** Where should an approval gate be placed?
A. At the very start of the process
B. As late as possible, just before the irreversible action
C. After every AI step
D. At the end, after the email has been sent

**5.** A candidate saves ten hours a month and scores 9 on risk. The right decision is...
A. Build it fully automatic
B. Leave it alone
C. Build it with a human approval gate before the irreversible step
D. Reduce the value estimate

**6.** In a future-state map, what is the Automation lane?
A. A lane for the IT department
B. A new lane holding the steps a machine will do, with a named owner who answers when it fails
C. A lane that replaces all the human lanes
D. A lane that is only drawn once the automation is built

**7.** A trained person sorts incoming requests in seconds by reading them, but nobody can list all the exceptions. The step is...
A. Rule-based
B. AI-judged
C. Human-only
D. Not a candidate

**8.** The confirmation email to the employee always has the same wording with the dates filled in. The simplest suitable approach is...
A. An AI drafting step reviewed by HR
B. A fixed template filled by a rule-based step
C. Keeping it human-only
D. Removing the confirmation

**9.** An automation would save 6 minutes per run and runs 60 times a month. What is its value in this course's scoring?
A. 360 hours a month
B. 60 hours a month
C. 10 hours a month
D. 6 hours a month

**10.** An AI step drafts a reply in place of 5 minutes of writing, but a person spends 2 minutes checking every draft. How many minutes per run count as saved?
A. 5
B. 3
C. 2
D. 7

**11.** A candidate's risk total is 5. Using the course's rule of thumb, it should...
A. Run fully automatically with no record
B. Run, with a person notified who can undo it
C. Stop at an approval gate every time
D. Never be automated

**12.** On the prioritisation matrix, a low-value, high-risk candidate should be...
A. Built first
B. Built with a gate
C. Batched later
D. Left alone, improving the human step with a better summary or checklist

**13.** Which ROI statement would a manager most likely trust?
A. "11.3 hours a month."
B. "It will transform the department."
C. "About 8 to 12 hours a month, assuming 40 requests and 12 to 18 minutes saved each."
D. "Hundreds of hours a year."

**14.** Two approval gates appear one after the other before the same action, with the same evidence. What is the problem?
A. None: more gates mean more control
B. The second gate is a RUBBER stamp waiting to happen
C. Gates must always be at the start
D. The AI step should approve instead

**15.** The risk challenger bot raises one of your risk scores. What should you do?
A. Accept it, because the AI knows best
B. Ignore it, because AI reviewers are unreliable
C. Decide whether that failure can happen in your process, then change the score or write why not in Notes
D. Lower the value to compensate

## Answer key

| # | Answer | Why | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | Rule-based means the logic is fully specifiable. Anything with "usually" or unlisted exceptions is AI-judged or human-only. | Workflow mapping |
| 2 | C | Automating a meaningless approval preserves the waste. Remove it, or keep only the exception cases for a person. | Workflow mapping |
| 3 | C | Risk is scored on reversibility, money, people and legal/data. Popularity may affect adoption but not risk. | Controls and human oversight |
| 4 | B | Late placement lets the automation prepare a complete proposal and stops exactly before the action that cannot be undone. | Controls and human oversight |
| 5 | C | High value, high risk: automate the preparation and keep the decision human. That is the "build with a gate" quadrant. | Controls and human oversight |
| 6 | B | Steps migrate into the Automation lane; what stays in the human lanes is the point. Every lane, including this one, has an owner. | Workflow mapping |
| 7 | B | Fast for a trained reader, but the exceptions cannot be written as a rule: the definition of AI-judged. | Workflow patterns |
| 8 | B | Use the simplest thing that works. Identical text is a template, not a model. | Workflow patterns |
| 9 | D | Value = minutes saved per run x runs per month / 60 = 6 x 60 / 60 = 6 hours. | Workflow mapping |
| 10 | B | Count only the minutes the automation removes: 5 minutes of writing minus 2 minutes of review. | Workflow mapping |
| 11 | B | 0 to 3 fully automatic; 4 to 7 runs with a person notified who can undo; 8 to 12 stops at a gate. | Controls and human oversight |
| 12 | D | Not worth the risk. Give the person a better summary or checklist instead of automating. | Workflow mapping |
| 13 | C | A range with its assumptions is honest and survives challenge; it can be checked against real numbers later. | Workflow mapping |
| 14 | B | One gate per irreversible action. A second identical gate trains people to approve without reading. | Controls and human oversight |
| 15 | C | The bot only knows what you pasted. Verify each challenge against your process and record the reason either way. | Controls and human oversight |

## Notes for the instructor

- Questions 8, 10 and 14 are the ones participants most often get wrong: they reach for AI when a template is enough, count review time as saved, and think more gates mean more safety.
- For a five-minute in-session check, use questions 1 to 5. For homework, use 6 to 15.
